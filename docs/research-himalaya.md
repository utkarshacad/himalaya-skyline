# The Himalaya as a grid

Research notes from 14 November 2026. Almost every textbook organises the Himalaya along two axes. Combining them gives a 4×4 grid that works as a memory device and as the game's progress map.

## North to south: parallel belts

Each boundary is a major fault.

| Belt | Also called | Indian examples | Southern boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trans-Himalaya | Tibetan Himalaya | Karakoram, Ladakh Range, Zanskar, Kailash (Tibet) | Indus–Tsangpo suture (the India–Asia collision seam; northern limit of the Himalaya proper) |
| Greater Himalaya | Himadri | Nun-Kun, Nanda Devi, Kamet, Kangchenjunga, Everest | South Tibetan Detachment above, **Main Central Thrust** below |
| Lesser Himalaya | Himachal | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie, Nag Tibba, Mahabharat Lekh | **Main Boundary Thrust** |
| Shivalik | Outer Himalaya | Jammu Hills, Dehradun Shivaliks, Churia Hills, Dafla/Miri/Abor/Mishmi hills | **Main Frontal Thrust** |

- **Duns** are flat valleys between the Shivalik and Lesser Himalaya: Dehradun, Kalka Dun, Kiyarda Dun, Jammu Dun.
- The Shivaliks are 10–50 km wide and about 900–1,100 m high, made of gravel and alluvium.

## West to east: river-cut sections

From Sir Sidney Burrard's classification, taught in NCERT and UPSC material:

| Section | Between | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Punjab (Kashmir & Himachal) Himalaya | Indus – Sutlej | ~560 km | Karakoram, Ladakh, Zanskar, Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar; height falls east to west |
| Kumaon Himalaya | Sutlej – Kali | ~320 km | Uttarakhand; Nanda Devi, Kamet, Trisul; Mussoorie and Nag Tibba ranges; duns |
| Nepal Himalaya | Kali – Teesta | ~800 km | Highest section: Everest, Kangchenjunga, Makalu, Dhaulagiri, Annapurna; Kathmandu and Pokhara are old lake beds; the Shivalik almost vanishes east of the Gandak |
| Assam Himalaya | Teesta – Brahmaputra (Dihang) | ~750 km | Lower; very steep southern slopes; Lesser Himalaya very narrow; Kangto, Namcha Barwa |

Section lengths vary slightly between sources (PMF IAS gives 720 km for the eastern section).

East of the Dihang gorge the range turns sharply south as the **Purvanchal**: Patkai Bum, Naga, Manipur and Mizo hills, and the Barail range.

## Ends, ages and speeds

- The Himalaya is about **2,400 km** long, between two "syntaxes": **Nanga Parbat** in the west and **Namcha Barwa** in the east.
- Nanga Parbat has the fastest uplift in the belt, nearly **10 mm a year**.
- The collision began about **50–65 million years ago**. India moved at 18–19.5 cm a year before slowing to about 4.5 cm a year around 55 Ma. Convergence today is about **17 mm a year**.
- About **2,500 km** of crust has been shortened since the collision.

## Rivers older than the mountains

**Antecedent rivers** kept their courses while the land rose and cut gorges straight through the ranges: the Indus, Sutlej, Kosi, Subansiri and Brahmaputra. That makes rivers natural **section borders** and natural **"handrails"** for the game.

## A mountaineering filing system

- The Alpine Club divides the Eastern Alps into **75 numbered groups**.
- The Himalayan Journal's 1986 classification (Adams Carter) does the same for the Himalaya, using **major rivers and passes as boundaries**. It deliberately avoids grouping by watershed because that causes confusion.
- Its Indian Garhwal and Kumaon groups are the Eastern Kumaon Himalaya, Nanda Devi Group, Kamet Group, Gangotri Group and Bandarpunch Range. This could feed a finer-grained grid later.
